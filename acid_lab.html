Generator

Waveform picks the oscillator. Classic blends saw and square with the Saw · Sq knob; the others are variations on the acid wave, each shaped by the Shape knob: TB saw bends the ramp the way the real circuit's capacitor does, TB square droops across each half like the 303's transistor-made square, Narrow pulse sets the width, Pulse-width sweep moves the width once every two beats, Detuned saw sets how far apart the two saws are, Sync saw sets the hard-sync ratio (the screaming, tearing kind). The layer follows the waveform (Classic for detuned and sync).

Signal path: saw/square oscillator (plus an optional layer an octave or a fifth away) → optional overdrive into the filter → ladder filter (resonance through a high-pass, like the 303's) → accent and amp → drive → tone → crush. The sub runs on its own path so the 303's low cut never thins it. Accent opens the filter with its own decay; Sweep sets how fast the accent circuit charges, so accents close together stack up into the classic “wow”. Tracking, Filter FM, Attack, Accent decay, the long Slide range and the Muffler come from the Devil Fish mod. Tweak LFO moves the cutoff slowly in time with the beat, like a hand on the knob, in both Pattern and Song mode. Bandpass with stacked drive and a 100 Hz low cut is the modern hard-techno acid sound.

The arp takes its notes from the 303 line, so it always stays in key: “Chord from each note” stacks scale notes on whichever note the pattern is playing, “The bar's notes” uses every pitch in the bar. Styles match Ableton's Arpeggiator. Steps and Distance transpose each new cycle by scale steps (Ableton's Shifted mode). Gate over 100% plays legato, which on a 303 means slides. The step lanes work like Serum 2's arp: each tick moves one step along, with its own octave, accent, length, ratchet, chance and slide. Lane cells: click to toggle or cycle, drag the bars up or down.

The song is built in phrases. Each phrase plays an idea (A is your pattern, B a new melody on its rhythm, C a lifted or arpeggiated version, D a breakdown), varies it bar by bar by the Evolve amount and can end with a fill. Everything is grown from the Song #, so the same number and settings always give the same song.
Signal path: 303 → low cut → pump → squash → motion → delay and reverb (sends) → width → output, with the sub and the drum guide added after the effects. Duck dips the echoes while notes play so the line stays clear and the echoes fill the gaps. Squash is OTT-style three-band compression blended in parallel. Width adds a side signal that cancels in mono, so the low end stays centred. The drum guide is synthesized here, never sampled. Choose the beat, then the kit: the clean synth kit, a vintage break kit modelled on the dusty 60s/70s funk drums behind the classic breaks (tuned, ringing snare, roomy kick, ride and crash), or an 808 (long boom kick, metallic hats, cowbell, maracas). The break beats follow the classic patterns (Amen, Think, Hot Pants, Funky Drummer, Impeach), some across two or four bars. Shaker adds 16th-note shaker over any beat, Crunch adds old-sampler grit (bit-reduced and darker), Room adds a small room around the kit. Recordings capture the drums as you hear them; exports leave them out unless you tick them in Save and export.

Audio engine

A larger buffer stops crackles on busy machines, at the cost of a slightly later response. It applies from the next sound.

MIDI: accents are velocity 127, other notes 80; slides are written as overlapping notes, which is how TB-303 clones and most mono synths read a slide. With filter moves on, the song's cutoff and resonance automation is written as CC 74 and CC 71. The drum guide goes on channel 10 when it is included. WAV exports render with the same engine you hear.
