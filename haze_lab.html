Intensity sets how far the effect dice go: Subtle stays clean, Wild reaches for heavy feedback, crush, fast phasing and hard gating.

Signal path: oscillators → filter 1 → filter 2 → chord and pattern levels → pump → gate (dry) → tape → chorus → phaser → granular (grains are laid on top) → delay and reverb → gate (everything) → auto-pan → output (limiter and soft clip). Ear candy joins before the delay and reverb. Gate steps: click to toggle, drag up or down for level, drag across to paint, right-click to silence. Delay time and Pump follow the tempo. Shimmer feeds an octave-up copy of each note into the reverb only.

Ear candy plays with the progression when you press Play. Every hit lands on the tempo’s sixteenth-note grid (Swing pushes the off-beats late), and risers and reverse swells are timed to finish exactly on a beat or the end of a phrase. The arrangement is built in phrases: a phrase repeats, morphs a little each time by the Evolve amount, switches to a second idea every 8 phrases, and every fourth phrase ends with a fill. Crushed sounds go through a bit and sample-rate crusher; Echo throws them into the tempo delay even when the Delay mix is off.

Each route moves a destination by up to ± its amount of the knob’s range, and never past the knob’s ends. Several routes on one destination add up.

Edit any chord with the row under the timeline. Locked chords stay put when you roll new chords (and so does the key); the others are re-picked from the mood.

Audio engine

A larger buffer gives the computer more time to work out each slice of sound, which stops crackles on busy or battery-saving machines, at the cost of keys responding a little later. The change applies from the next note.

A loop export renders the progression once and folds the tails back onto the start. Free-running LFOs and other slow movement are fitted to whole cycles of the loop so it repeats without a seam, which can nudge a very slow LFO’s rate. One-shots hold for the chosen time and then ring out. “Generated pattern, full length” renders the pattern from the keyboard section over its whole length, with the chords cycling underneath unless it plays alone. MIDI exports the chords, and when the pattern is on, the whole pattern on a second track. Record (top bar) captures a live performance instead. Play volume never affects exports or recordings.
